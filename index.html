Question 01 · The license

The Suite is an add-on to Microsoft 365 E3

What the license is, what it needs and what it costs.

2 sources on this pageLast verified 5 November 2026

Short answer

Microsoft 365 E3 already includes core Purview. The Suite adds automation, reach and depth on top: auto-labeling, DLP for Teams, devices and Copilot, insider-risk tools, premium eDiscovery and longer audit retention, for each licensed user.[1, 2]

+ Microsoft Purview Suite

Add-on, licensed per user

Auto-labelingDLP for Teams, devices, CopilotInsider RiskCommunication ComplianceeDiscovery & Audit (Premium)Records management

Microsoft 365 E3

Core Purview for every licensed user

Manual labelsDLP for email and filesRetentioneDiscovery (Standard)Audit (Standard)Compliance Manager

E3 + Suite = the Purview capabilities of Microsoft 365 E5[2]

What it is
Formerly Microsoft 365 E5 Compliance. Microsoft says the rename changes neither features nor price.[1]
Prerequisite
Microsoft 365 E3, or Office 365 E3 plus Enterprise Mobility + Security E3.[1]
Licensed per user
One license for each user whose data you protect or manage with it.[1]
US list price
USD 12.00 per user per month, paid yearly. Your agreement price may differ.[1]

Question 02 · Users without the Suite

Without a Suite license: can and can't

What Microsoft 365 E3 users without the Suite keep, and what they miss.

2 sources on this pageLast verified 5 November 2026

Short answer

The essentials. Users label by hand, DLP guards email and files, retention keeps or deletes, eDiscovery searches and holds, and audit looks back 180 days. What's missing is automation, DLP for Teams, devices and Copilot, insider-risk tools and premium investigation.[2, 3]

Scope

Here, “without a license” means Microsoft 365 E3 without the Suite. Someone with no Microsoft 365 license isn't licensed for any of these capabilities.

✓You can, with Microsoft 365 E3

  • Label and encrypt by handManual, default and mandatory labels; encryption
  • Prevent leaks in email and filesDLP for Exchange, SharePoint and OneDrive
  • Keep or delete on a scheduleRetention policies and manual retention labels
  • Search, hold and exporteDiscovery (Standard): search, cases, holds, export
  • Investigate activityAudit (Standard) with 180-day retention
  • Track compliance postureCompliance Manager (assessments vary by license)

✕You can't, without the Suite

  • Label automaticallyNo auto-labeling in apps or as service policies
  • Extend DLP beyond email and filesNo DLP for Teams, devices or Copilot processing
  • Detect people-driven riskNo Insider Risk Management or Communication Compliance
  • Manage formal recordsNo disposition review or regulatory records
  • Run deep investigationsNo eDiscovery (Premium) review sets or analytics
  • Keep audit longerNo 1-year audit retention or crucial-event logging

Sources for both lists: Microsoft licensing guidance for Purview and the Microsoft Purview service description.[2, 3]

Question 03 · The rule

Who counts as benefiting

Microsoft's examples of who needs a license once a Suite feature applies.

1 source on this pageLast verified 5 November 2026

Microsoft's examples are not exhaustive; the rule behind them is that any user who benefits from the service needs a license.[3]

Needs a Suite license

  • Owners of mailboxes, OneDrive, Teams chats, devicesThese belong to a user, so that user needs the license
  • Owners and members of shared spacesSharePoint sites, Microsoft 365 Groups, Teams channels under a Suite policy
  • People with Purview rolesAdmins and investigators assigned a role in the Purview portal
  • eDiscovery (Premium) custodians and adminsBoth the person whose data is analyzed and the admin doing it
  • Shared or resource mailboxesWhen the feature needs an E5-level license, such as the Suite
  • Information Barriers segmentsEvery user in a segment assigned to a barrier policy

No license needed

  • Site visitors, view-only usersRead access to a covered site or team
  • Inactive mailboxesRetained mailboxes of people who left
  • Shared mailboxes, many featuresUnless the feature is E5-level

Practical tip

Keep each shared team or site either fully licensed or outside Suite policy scope.

Question 03 · Compliance

Enforcement varies; the licensing rule doesn't

What the service enforces, and what you must scope yourself.

2 sources on this pageLast verified 5 November 2026

“You must appropriately license the use of a service regardless of technical enforcement.”

Microsoft licensing guidance for Purview[2]

The service enforces some limits

  • Audit (Premium)One-year retention and crucial events apply only to licensed users.[3]
  • Customer KeyFor multiple workloads, needs at least as many licenses as users with Exchange or Teams licenses, or encryption reverts to default. 500 of 1,000 isn't enough.[3]
  • License checksSome services check for a license, or let admins limit access to licensed users.[2]
  • Future checksServices that don't enforce today could start in future.[2]

You must scope the rest

  • Can't always be limitedSome tenant services can't yet limit benefits to specific users; licenses are still required.[3]
  • Defaults are tenant-wideFor example, Teams chats and channels are a DLP location for all users until you include or exclude users or groups.[3]
  • No multiplexingPooling or rerouting access doesn't reduce the licenses you need.[2]
  • No license rotationA license can't be reassigned within 90 days of its last assignment, except to cover a user's absence.[2]

Question 03 · How to run it

Running a 500/500 split

Recommended practice for a partial rollout that stays inside the licensing rules.

3 sources on this pageLast verified 5 November 2026

Recommended practice This is guidance, not a Microsoft requirement.

  1. 1

    Pick by data risk

    Sensitive-data roles, privileged admins and investigators first.

  2. 2

    Group and license

    One security group; group-based licensing assigns the Suite.

  3. 3

    Scope Suite policies

    Include only that group; DLP and Insider Risk policies support user and group scoping.[3, 9]

  4. 4

    Fix shared spaces

    License all owners and members of covered sites and teams.[3]

  5. 5

    Review regularly

    Joiners, leavers and new sites; reassignment has a 90-day rule.[2]

Known gap

Content created by the other 500 isn't auto-labeled, and their activity isn't covered by insider-risk policies. That is the honest cost of a partial rollout.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Short, sourced answers to common customer questions.

4 sources on this pageLast verified 5 November 2026
Can we license the Suite for only some of our users?

Yes. The Suite is licensed per user, so you can license a subset. Every user who benefits from a Suite feature needs a license, so scope Suite policies to the licensed users.[1, 3]

Do users without the Suite lose anything they have today?

No. They keep every capability Microsoft 365 E3 includes. Suite features just must not be applied to them.[2, 3]

If a service doesn't technically block unlicensed users, are we covered?

No. Microsoft's guidance says you must license the use of a service regardless of technical enforcement. Some services check licenses today, and others could start in future.[2]

Can we move licenses between people as needs change?

Yes, but not within 90 days of the last assignment, except to cover a user's absence. Pooling or rerouting access (multiplexing) doesn't reduce the licenses you need.[2]

Do people who only view a covered site or team need a license?

No. Visitors and view-only users don't. Owners and members do, once a Suite feature applies to that site or team.[3]

Can we buy only part of the Suite?

Yes. Three Microsoft 365 E5 subset add-ons each cover part of it: Information Protection and Governance, Insider Risk Management, and eDiscovery and Audit. If users need two or more, compare with the full Suite.[2]

Does the Suite cover non-Microsoft AI apps or data outside Microsoft 365?

Not through per-user licensing. Those capabilities are pay-as-you-go through an Azure subscription. Microsoft 365 Copilot experiences aren't charged under pay-as-you-go.[6]

What about frontline workers or Business Premium?

Frontline workers on Microsoft 365 F1/F3 can add the Purview Suite or F5 subsets. Business Premium (up to 300 users) has Purview Suite for Business Premium.[2, 3]
