Question 01

What changes with a Purview Suite license?

Click for the short answer

Short answer

Microsoft 365 E3 already includes core Purview. The Suite adds automation, reach and depth on top: auto-labeling, DLP for Teams, devices and Copilot, insider-risk tools, premium eDiscovery and longer audit retention, for each licensed user.[1,2]

Every Microsoft 365 E3 user already has core Purview. The real question is what the extra layer adds, and for whom.[2]

Question 02

Without a Suite license, what still works?

Click for the short answer

Short answer

The essentials. Users label by hand, DLP guards email and files, retention keeps or deletes, eDiscovery searches and holds, and audit looks back 180 days. What's missing is automation, DLP for Teams, devices and Copilot, insider-risk tools and premium investigation.[2,3]

Here, “without a license” means Microsoft 365 E3 without the Suite. Someone with no Microsoft 365 license isn't licensed for any of these capabilities.

Question 03

1,000 employees, 500 licenses: what about the rest?

Click for the short answer

Short answer

They keep everything Microsoft 365 E3 includes. Suite features must be scoped to the 500 licensed users: Microsoft requires a license for every user who benefits, even where a service can't technically limit it.[3,2]

Question 03 · Compliance

Enforcement varies; the licensing rule doesn't

“You must appropriately license the use of a service regardless of technical enforcement.”

Microsoft licensing guidance for Purview[2]

The service enforces some limits

Audit (Premium)

One-year retention and crucial events apply only to licensed users.[3]

Customer Key

For multiple workloads, needs at least as many licenses as users with Exchange or Teams licenses, or encryption reverts to default. 500 of 1,000 isn't enough.[3]

License checks

Some services check for a license, or let admins limit access to licensed users.[2]

Future checks

Services that don't enforce today could start in future.[2]

You must scope the rest

Can't always be limited

Some tenant services can't yet limit benefits to specific users; licenses are still required.[3]

Defaults are tenant-wide

For example, Teams chats and channels are a DLP location for all users until you include or exclude users or groups.[3]

No multiplexing

Pooling or rerouting access doesn't reduce the licenses you need.[2]

No license rotation

A license can't be reassigned within 90 days of its last assignment, except to cover a user's absence.[2]

Question 03 · How to run it

Running a 500/500 split

Recommended practice, not a Microsoft requirement, that keeps a partial rollout inside the licensing rules.

1

Pick by data risk

Sensitive-data roles, privileged admins, investigators

2

Group and license

One security group; group-based licensing assigns the Suite

3

Scope Suite policies

Include only that group; DLP and Insider Risk policies support user and group scoping[3,9]

4

Fix shared spaces

License all owners and members of covered sites and teams[3]

5

Review regularly

Joiners, leavers, new sites; reassignment has a 90-day rule[2]

Known gap

Content created by the other 500 isn't auto-labeled, and their activity isn't covered by insider-risk policies. That is the honest cost of a partial rollout.

Key takeaways

Four things to remember

  1. 1No Suite doesn't mean no protection: Microsoft 365 E3 already includes core Purview.[2]
  2. 2The Suite adds automation, reach and depth, licensed per user.[1]
  3. 3Every user a Suite feature touches needs a license; scope policies to match.[3]
  4. 4Partial licensing works, with known gaps. Decide by data risk, not headcount.
